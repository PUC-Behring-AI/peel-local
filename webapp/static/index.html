Phase 3: collocation-pair selection

    
These term pairs actually co-occur in the source text (found by scanning for
       real proximity, not guessed). Pick one or more to compare -- via a keyword-in-context
       concordance -- between the source and each condensation summary. Rows marked "possibly
       confounded" involve a disproportionately frequent term, so the co-occurrence may just be
       base rate -- you can still pick a flagged pair if the specific pairing looks meaningful.

    

      How these candidates and flags are chosen
      

        Candidates: for every Phase 1 cluster, its top 6 stems/n-grams by how
        often they actually occur in the source form the term pool. Every pair of pool terms drawn
        from two different clusters is checked for real proximity -- a pair only becomes a
        candidate if the two terms actually sit within 5 tokens of each other somewhere in the
        source at least once. Candidates are ranked by that co-occurrence count, most first, and
        capped at the top 20 shown here.
      

      

        "Possibly confounded": unrelated to how often the pair co-occurs. A pair is
        flagged when the more frequent of its two terms occurs, on its own, at least
        5× the median occurrence count across the whole term pool -- a term that common in
        the source will sit near almost anything, so a co-occurrence count involving it is weaker
        evidence of a real contextual link, not necessarily a wrong one. It's a caveat for your
        judgment, not a verdict: pick a flagged pair if the specific pairing still looks meaningful.
