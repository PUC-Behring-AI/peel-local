Advanced settings (Phase 1 tuning)

Percentile cutoff on the frequency-ranked stem vocabulary. Higher = a stricter bar = fewer, more frequent stems (e.g. 0.75 keeps only the top 25% most-frequent stems).

Percentile cutoff on each stem's OWN derived-word frequency ranking (0 = keep every derived word, i.e. no filtering). Higher = fewer, more frequent derived words considered per stem (e.g. 0.75 keeps only the top 25% most-frequent words derived from each stem) -- trims how many distinct words (like "observation"/"observes"/"observed" all under stem "observ") reach GlossBERT/flagged-term review. Always keeps at least one word per stem.

Tries several frequency-percentile values -- a larger value is a stricter cutoff and keeps fewer stems (matching the usual sense of a percentile cutoff, like "90th percentile" meaning "top 10%"), shown here uncapped by "Max stems" above so you see each candidate's true size -- and reports, for each, how many sentences contain a top-stem word and how many clear a few informativeness thresholds (reusing Phase 2's sentence-scoring), so you can pick one -- or type your own -- on the next screen, overriding "Frequency percentile"/"Max stems" above. Cheap: no GlossBERT/clustering run yet.

A ceiling, not a guarantee: this many candidate definitions are shown per flagged word at most. WordNet's actual sense count varies a lot by word and part of speech (e.g. "call" as a verb has 28 senses, but "human" as an adjective has only 3) -- a flagged word with few senses for its part of speech will show fewer candidates than this, no matter how high you set it.

If a word occurs more than once among the sampled sentences and GlossBERT predicts a different sense each time, it would otherwise show up as more than one flagged-review item. Enabling this merges all of a word's sampled occurrences into one combined context and asks GlossBERT once per word instead of once per occurrence.

Phase 3: collocation-pair selection

    
These term pairs actually co-occur in the source text (found by scanning for
       real proximity, not guessed). Pick one or more to compare -- via a keyword-in-context
       concordance -- between the source and each condensation summary. Rows marked "possibly
       confounded" involve a disproportionately frequent term, so the co-occurrence may just be
       base rate -- you can still pick a flagged pair if the specific pairing looks meaningful.

    

      How these candidates and flags are chosen
      

        Candidates: for every Phase 1 cluster, its top 6 stems/n-grams by how
        often they actually occur in the source form the term pool. Every pair of pool terms drawn
        from two different clusters is checked for real proximity -- a pair only becomes a
        candidate if the two terms actually sit within 5 tokens of each other somewhere in the
        source at least once. Candidates are ranked by that co-occurrence count, most first, and
        capped at the top 20 shown here.
      

      

        "Possibly confounded": unrelated to how often the pair co-occurs. A pair is
        flagged when the more frequent of its two terms occurs, on its own, at least
        5× the median occurrence count across the whole term pool -- a term that common in
        the source will sit near almost anything, so a co-occurrence count involving it is weaker
        evidence of a real contextual link, not necessarily a wrong one. It's a caveat for your
        judgment, not a verdict: pick a flagged pair if the specific pairing still looks meaningful.
